## 2026-09-07

- **Scope.** Build from scratch. Nothing pre-existing except the Vite scaffold in `web/` and the research dossier.
- **Framing.** Inspection targeting, not culprit attribution. The system produces leads; MARPOL enforcement confirms by oil sample match on board. Cite SkyTruth Cerulean as prior art ourselves.
- **Roles.** Two only: `admin` and `officer`. Viewer role dropped. No public signup; admin provisions accounts.
- **Auth.** Self-built. bcrypt cost 12, HS256 JWT, 12 h expiry, localStorage, Bearer header. slowapi rate limit on login. Refresh tokens and OTP deferred.
- **Python.** 3.13 for backend and ML. 3.14 rejected because opencv, rasterio and onnxruntime wheels lag.
- **Model serving.** ONNX + onnxruntime in the API container. PyTorch only for training (Colab T4).
- **Model.** Custom U-Net, 5 classes (sea, oil, look-alike, ship, land), trained on Krestenitis et al. 2019. Class-weighted CE + Dice. Report per-class IoU on the 110-image test split.
- **Fallback.** OpenCV dark-spot heuristic when the model is unavailable. Always labelled `engine: "heuristic"` in API and UI.
- **Map.** Cesium for the app globe. `cobe` kept for the landing hero only.
- **Database.** MongoDB Atlas M0 with GeoJSON + 2dsphere. PostGIS considered and rejected for free tier and document flexibility.
- **Drift.** First-order Lagrangian backtrack: current + 3 % wind, Open-Meteo Marine + Forecast, three origin ellipses at t-6h, t-12h, t-24h. OpenDrift is the stated upgrade path.
- **AIS.** AISStream live feed is real. Historical tracks for the demo incident are a generated scenario, labelled as such.
- **Ranking.** Eight weighted features, 0 to 100 score, tiers: prime suspect ≥ 65, person of interest 35 to 64, cleared < 35. Weights in one config file.
- **Hosting.** Vercel (web), Render Docker (API + ML), MongoDB Atlas.
- **Frontend folder** stays named `web/` (the scaffold's name) rather than `frontend/`.
- **Repo** is the source of truth so work continues across Claude accounts and teammates' machines.

## 2026-09-07 (later)

- **Officers are assigned to watch zones.** Users carry `region` (one of ICG West / North-West / East / North-East / A&N) and `zone_ids[]`. Watch zones carry `region`. Admin sets both when creating an officer and can change them later, including zones outside the officer's home region for temporary postings.
- **Assignment is a workload filter, not a permission wall.** Alerts, "my zones" default in the zone selector, and "my cases" use it. Every officer can read every incident nationally. Admin vs officer remains the only hard permission boundary.
- **Incident ownership.** An incident is owned by the officer who confirmed it. Admin can reassign. Others in the region can add notes.
- **Detection verification step.** Before an incident exists the officer must tag the detection: confirm as oil spill (opens the incident), dismiss as look-alike (reason: wind shadow, algal bloom, rain cell, low wind, other), or mark uncertain (second-opinion queue). Every tag is stored with the tile as a training label for the Indian-waters look-alike model.
- **Verification signals shown on the console.** Look-alike vs oil pixel share, wind speed at acquisition (flag < 3 m/s), previous-scene toggle for the same tile, distance to nearest shipping lane and nearest AIS vessel, polygon length-to-width ratio (> 4:1 reads as a discharge trail).
- **Model selection.** Train three candidates on the same loader, loss and 256 px tiles: (1) custom U-Net from scratch in our own code, (2) U-Net with a pretrained ResNet-34 encoder via segmentation_models_pytorch, (3) DeepLabV3+ with a pretrained ResNet-50 encoder. Score all three on the 110-image Krestenitis test split (per-class IoU, mIoU, params, CPU inference ms). The best mIoU with acceptable CPU latency is exported to ONNX and served; the other two stay as rows in `ml/metrics.json` and appear in the comparison table on the Detection Console and in the report. Only one model runs in production. If time is short, train (1) and (2) first; (3) can be added later without touching the app.
- **Quality bar.** Production grade throughout: typed schemas, tests, env-driven config, Docker, CI green, no demo-only shortcuts left in code paths that ship. Build one component at a time to "done when" acceptance.
- **Detection upgrades (weeks 3 to 4).** (1) Ship-at-slick-head: ship-class pixels at the head of an elongated slick are geolocated and matched to the nearest AIS position at acquisition; a match adds a top-weighted ranking feature, no match flags a dark vessel. (2) Context filters shown as lines in the result panel: wind < 3 m/s at acquisition, downwind of coast within a few km (Natural Earth coastline), same patch present on previous pass, no AIS traffic within 30 km in 24 h. (3) Uncertainty overlay from softmax entropy. Whole-scene inference uses 256 px tiles with 32 px overlap and test-time augmentation; dB calibration and speckle filter identical in training and serving. Volume estimate via Bonn Agreement thickness codes shown as a caveated range. Roadmap: EOS-04 / NISAR tiles, dual-pol once Indian labels exist.
- **Incident upgrades (weeks 6 to 7).** (1) Close-out outcome (confirmed by sample / boarded and cleared / not boarded) stored per incident and used to report tier precision and re-fit weights. (2) Intercept card for the top suspect: projected positions at +6/+12/+24 h, next port and ETA, pre-filled Port State Control request. (3) Multi-scene linking: a new detection overlapping an open incident's drifted position attaches to it; incident shows slick evolution and drift-prediction error. Roadmap: probabilistic drift cloud, versioned ranking, repeat-offender view, alerts with escalation, officer overrides with reasons, GeoJSON/KML export.
- **Health `model` field** reports `unet` when an ONNX file is present, otherwise `heuristic` (the fallback is always available, so "missing" was misleading). The console shows which engine answered on every result.
- **Sample tiles** are synthetic, watermarked placeholders until real Sentinel-1 tiles are exported from Copernicus Browser. They carry `synthetic: true` and the UI labels them. Never present them as imagery.
- **Drift + ranking implemented as designed.** Weather from Open-Meteo forecast/archive + marine endpoints; unreachable service degrades to a climatological fallback flagged `weather_source: fallback` and shown in the UI. Ranking reads `ais_positions` (live or scenario, indistinguishable), interpolates tracks at 5-minute steps so dark segments still register spatial/temporal fit, and evaluates each zone in a time window of ±max(1.5 h, 0.35 × hours_before). Candidate cut-off is 2.5 ellipse radii.
- **Seed runs the real pipeline.** The demo incident is no longer hand-inserted. `scripts/seed_db.py` segments the bundled tile, fetches the real weather for the acquisition night, backtracks the origin zones, lays the scenario ship tracks around those zones (guilty tanker through the t-12h zone on the slick axis, gap 12.6→11.9 h before acquisition), and calls the same `create_incident` service the API uses. Reason: with real weather the drift went east, not south-west as the hand-written tracks assumed, and the pipeline honestly found zero candidates. Tracks relative to computed zones keep the demo consistent on any date.

## 2026-09-07 (evening)

- **`cobe` is now used on the Dashboard too, not just the landing hero.** This supersedes the earlier "cobe kept for the landing hero only" line. The Dashboard gains a "Where the slicks are" panel: a dark interactive globe with one clickable dot per incident, coloured by the leading candidate's tier (prime = red, person of interest = amber, not ranked = teal) and sized by slick area. Clicking a dot selects that locality and flies the globe to centre it; the pane beside it shows zone, area, confidence, detection time, centroid to 4 dp, ships in zone now, and the leading candidate, with a link into the investigation. Selection is two-way: a crosshair button on each row of "Open incidents" selects the same slick on the globe. Cesium remains the decision for the full app map (Live Map), where real basemap imagery, drift ellipses and AIS tracks are needed; cobe is the at-a-glance national picture, which is a different job and much cheaper.
- **Marker screen positions are read from cobe's own anchor divs.** cobe v2 appends a 1 px anchor div per `id`-bearing marker to the wrapper it injects around the canvas, carrying `left`/`top` as percentages. Reading those is exact and avoids re-deriving its projection. Only front/back visibility is computed locally (camera-space z sign, which needs no calibration). The component therefore renders real `<button>` markers rather than relying on CSS Anchor Positioning, which is Chromium-only and cannot receive clicks.
- **Globe rebuilds are gated on a marker content signature, not array identity.** TanStack Query returns a fresh array on every refetch; `createGlobe` at 22 000 map samples is expensive, and cobe's `destroy()` removes its anchor divs but *not* the wrapper div it injects, so rebuilding on every refetch would nest wrappers indefinitely. The effect keys on a signature of marker id/location/size/colour, live data reaches the render loop through a ref, and teardown unwraps cobe's wrapper.
